Hello App Review team, thank you for the detailed questions. Peep Math is an independent app made and maintained by me (Yongmin Lee), not from a template or an app generation service. Answers below.

1. What the app does
It is a math practice app that follows the school curriculum from counting (kindergarten) to grade 12 in 14 grade tracks with about 13,000 questions. Every question first shows a diagram drawn by the app (objects, clocks, fractions, number lines, charts, angles, balance scales, graphs), and 1,171 questions are answered by manipulating it (turning clock hands, painting fractions, dragging items into groups, building bar graphs, balancing equations). A scratchpad sits on top of the question for working by hand, progress is tracked per skill area, and mistakes go into a review list. It helps learners who get stuck on abstract symbols see the concept first, without ads, accounts or a network connection.

2. Intended users
Learners who want to practise or review school math step by step, from beginners to high-school level, and families who study together at home (a parent can turn correct answers into real pocket money through the on-device allowance wallet). The App Store age rating is 13+. The app and all questions are in 14 languages.

3. Gap it addresses
Most math apps cover a narrow age range, are text-only drills, or need accounts and subscriptions. Peep Math covers K-12 in one place, shows a diagram for every question, lets the learner manipulate it instead of only typing a number, has a scratchpad on the question, and works fully offline.

4. Beta testing
The Android version has been used by my family throughout development and went through a Google Play closed test. Feedback applied to this build: answer choices were too big and looked like a calculator → bubble choices and a chick that reacts; formulas looked like plain text → real formula rendering; lesson circles in the last row were cut off (reported with a photo) → layout fixed; the language could only be changed by changing the whole phone → in-app language picker; the status bar overlapped the first line on the newest phones → fixed. The iPhone version was tested on an iPhone 16 Pro and with 68 unit tests.

5. Suite
It is a standalone product. Under the same "Peep" chick brand I also publish Peep English (a separate English course), Peep Push (a sokoban puzzle game) and Peep Science (a physics puzzle game). Peep Math is the only math curriculum app; the others contain no math lessons.

6. Could it be part of another app?
Math and English started as one app and I split them on purpose: each subject is a full curriculum of its own (about 13,000 math questions here; alphabet tracing, listening, speaking with speech recognition and reading in Peep English), the combined app was heavy and confusing, learners wanted to open straight into their subject with separate progress, and Peep Math needs no permissions while Peep English needs the microphone.

7. Shared code with my other apps
Yes, with Peep English only: the in-house UI layer that draws the screens, and the local progress modules (on-device database, allowance wallet and shop, hearts, streaks, badges, levels), plus the chick mascot illustrations and sound effects. I wrote these myself for both apps. The question content, question screens, interactive math visuals and the scratchpad are unique to Peep Math. No code is shared with Peep Push or Peep Science; they only use the same chick mascot style.

8. Third-party code
None. The app uses only Apple frameworks; no third-party SDKs, analytics or ads.

9. Client or third party
No. The concept, branding, questions (from my own question generator), illustrations and sounds were made for this app, and it is submitted under my own account.
